# Commonwealth of Black America — Repository Governance

**FROM OUR HISTORY, WE BUILD OUR FUTURE.**

This repository is the public institutional archive of the Commonwealth of Black America (CBA). It records public constitutional drafts, governance standards, public registry entries, institutional proposals, policy development, and historical releases.

## 1. Authority of the Repository

The repository is a public record and drafting platform. It does not replace the Constitution, lawful votes of Foundational Citizens, decisions of the People's Assembly, rulings of the Constitutional Court, or decisions of a duly constituted Citizenship Commission.

A GitHub commit is evidence that a document existed in a particular form at a particular time. A commit alone does not ratify a constitutional amendment, admit a citizen, appropriate funds, confer office, or create governmental authority.

## 2. Controlling Public Working Draft

Until formal ratification, the document identified in the repository as the current Constitution — Vision Edition v2 is the controlling public working draft for repository organization and companion-document development.

Where an older document conflicts with the current public Constitution, the newer constitutional language controls the repository's working interpretation pending formal ratification.

## 3. Change Process

Material changes should follow this sequence where practicable:

1. Proposal or issue opened.
2. Draft language prepared on a branch.
3. Public or internal review as appropriate.
4. Constitutional and legal-consistency review.
5. Required Commonwealth approval or ratification.
6. Merge into the public archive.
7. CHANGELOG and release record updated.

Minor corrections such as spelling, formatting, broken links, or non-substantive clarifications may be merged without a full policy process.

## 4. Constitutional Changes

No GitHub contributor may represent a constitutional amendment as ratified unless it has completed the ratification process required by the Constitution.

Draft amendments should be clearly labeled **PROPOSED** until ratified.

## 5. Citizenship Records

The public registry may contain only the minimum information approved for public disclosure, such as:

- public registry number
- citizen name, where the citizen consents to publication
- classification
- enrollment or ratification date
- public status
- a non-sensitive eligibility summary

Do **not** upload birth certificates, government identification, residential addresses, signatures, private family information, raw DNA data, account numbers, Social Security numbers, or complete genealogical evidence files to this public repository.

Citizenship applications and supporting evidence belong in a separate secure system controlled by the citizenship-verification process.

## 6. Treasury and Monetary Records

Public Treasury documents may describe policy, budgets, audited totals, reserve rules, and approved economic programs. They must not expose banking credentials, account numbers, private customer information, authentication material, or regulated financial data.

C$ documents must accurately distinguish between a branded payment interface, United States dollar balances held through regulated partners, third-party stablecoin infrastructure, and any future legally authorized C$ issuance.

## 7. Public Safety and Justice

Repository documents must preserve the constitutional distinction between voluntary Commonwealth institutions and governmental powers requiring external legal authorization.

No repository document may be used to falsely claim arrest authority, criminal jurisdiction, diplomatic immunity, compulsory judicial authority, or other governmental powers that have not been lawfully granted.

## 8. External Engagement

The Commonwealth may document cultural, educational, economic, institutional, municipal, and international outreach. Public materials shall accurately describe present status while preserving the Commonwealth's lawful long-term aspirations.

## 9. Maintainers

Repository maintainers are custodians of the public record. Their technical ability to merge, edit, or delete files does not create constitutional authority beyond any office or role they separately hold under Commonwealth law.

## 10. Transparency

Material public changes should remain visible through Git history whenever practicable. Historical versions should be preserved through tags, releases, archived documents, or the commit history rather than silently rewritten.

## 11. Conduct

Contributors must not use the repository for threats, harassment, fraud, doxxing, impersonation, illegal activity, or publication of private personal records.

Good-faith disagreement about policy, constitutional design, strategy, economics, history, or institutional development is permitted.
