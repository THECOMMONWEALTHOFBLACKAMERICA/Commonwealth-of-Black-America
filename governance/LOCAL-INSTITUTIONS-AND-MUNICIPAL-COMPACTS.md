# Local Institutions and Municipal Compact Strategy

**Commonwealth of Black America**  
**Public Working Framework — August 8, 2026**

## Institution-First Strategy

The Commonwealth’s long-term strategy is:

> **Build → Demonstrate → Negotiate → Institutionalize**

The objective is to build sufficient institutional capacity that Foundational Black American communities can increasingly administer their own affairs and, where governmental authority is legally required, negotiate the delegation, recognition, funding, or shared exercise of that authority with the cities, states, school districts, public authorities, and other governments in which Commonwealth citizens live.

## National, Regional, and Local Structure

### National

The national Commonwealth establishes constitutional standards, citizenship rules, Treasury policy, foreign affairs, national economic strategy, election standards, and institution-wide safeguards.

### Regional

Regional Commonwealth Councils may coordinate multiple cities or communities, support Regional Tribunals, development funds, public-safety planning, shared services, infrastructure initiatives, and inter-local cooperation.

### Local

Local Commonwealth Chapters may administer lawful community programs, public meetings, cultural institutions, economic-development initiatives, food systems, educational projects, property, emergency assistance, mediation, data collection, and other functions authorized by Commonwealth law.

## Municipal Compact Strategy

When a Local or Regional Commonwealth institution demonstrates sufficient membership, democratic legitimacy, operational capacity, financial controls, professional administration, and public support, it may seek formal agreements with existing governments.

Potential legal forms include:

- contracts
- memoranda of understanding
- franchises
- grants
- service agreements
- commissions
- delegations
- cooperative agreements
- legislation
- other recognized governmental or contractual mechanisms

## Potential Areas of Agreement

Subject to applicable law, negotiated agreements may concern:

- education
- community development
- housing
- food security
- workforce development
- public-safety partnerships
- restorative justice
- mediation
- neighborhood services
- cultural affairs
- infrastructure
- emergency response
- data-sharing
- business development
- other public functions

## Community Safety Corps

The Commonwealth may establish a Community Safety Corps for lawful functions such as:

- neighborhood observation and presence
- community escorts
- de-escalation
- conflict intervention
- missing-person assistance
- event safety
- youth intervention
- emergency preparedness
- disaster relief
- first-aid coordination

Where private security, armed security, investigations, emergency medical services, or other regulated services are provided, all required licensing, training, insurance, and legal authority must be obtained.

Arrest powers, warrant authority, traffic enforcement, criminal-investigation powers, access to restricted government databases, compulsory detention, and other governmental police powers may be exercised only if lawfully granted by a competent government and only by properly qualified personnel.

## Regional Tribunals

The Commonwealth may establish local hearing bodies, Regional Tribunals, appellate panels, and a Constitutional Court for internal matters including:

- citizenship
- elections
- officer conduct
- Commonwealth contracts
- membership discipline
- disputes among Commonwealth institutions

Regional Tribunals may also provide voluntary mediation, consensual arbitration, restorative-justice conferences, and private dispute resolution where the parties consent or are bound by a lawful agreement.

Any enforcement outside Commonwealth institutions must use mechanisms recognized by applicable law.

The Commonwealth may later seek governmental agreements or legislation allowing its tribunals to receive referrals, administer diversion or restorative-justice programs, conduct recognized arbitration or administrative hearings, or perform other public functions.

Criminal conviction, imprisonment, compulsory subpoena, contempt powers against the general public, or involuntary jurisdiction shall not be exercised without lawful governmental authorization.

## Accountability

Any future Commonwealth public-safety or justice institution exercising delegated governmental power should be subject to safeguards including:

- civilian oversight
- published rules
- professional qualification standards
- conflicts-of-interest requirements
- complaint procedures
- independent investigation
- public data reporting
- due process
- judicial review
- use-of-force standards where applicable
- body-camera or comparable accountability requirements where applicable

## Core Limitation

The Commonwealth does not acquire coercive authority over non-consenting members of the public merely by declaring an internal office, ordinance, court, or agency.

Governmental power over the public must rest on valid law, contract, delegation, commissioning, consent, accreditation, or another recognized legal basis.
