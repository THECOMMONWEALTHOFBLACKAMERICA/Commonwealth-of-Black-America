# Founding Reserve and Commonwealth Dollar Framework

**Commonwealth of Black America**  
**Public Working Framework — August 8, 2026**

## 1. Treasury Purpose

The Commonwealth Treasury shall manage lawful dues, membership contributions, grants, donations, program revenue, enterprise distributions, investment income, and other authorized revenue under published budgets, segregation of duties, conflict-of-interest rules, public reporting, and independent review or audit.

## 2. Community Development Fund

The Community Development Fund is intended to support programs and capital projects including:

- Black community schools and educational programs
- community centers
- grocery stores and food cooperatives
- food banks
- housing and neighborhood development
- business formation and entrepreneurship
- technology
- health and wellness initiatives
- cultural and historical preservation
- other projects approved through Commonwealth governance

The Community Development Fund is separate from the Founding Reserve Fund.

## 3. One-Dollar Founding Reserve Contribution

Upon formal admission, each Foundational Citizen shall make a one-time **$1.00 Founding Reserve Contribution**.

The minimum book-value floor of the Founding Reserve Fund shall not intentionally fall below:

> **$1 × the number of formally admitted Foundational Citizens whose citizenship remains active.**

The reserve floor may be adjusted only to correct accounting error, fraud, or a legally required charge under the constitutional framework.

## 4. Reserve Separation

The Founding Reserve Fund shall be accounted for separately from:

- routine operating funds
- donor-restricted program funds
- customer payment balances
- stablecoin reserves
- development investment capital

The Founding Reserve is not ordinary operating money.

## 5. Gold Reserve Objective

As financial capacity grows, the Commonwealth shall pursue a long-term objective of holding allocated physical gold or other approved hard-reserve assets with market value equal to or greater than the constitutional Founding Reserve floor.

This is a reserve objective, not a promise that gold prices, U.S. dollars, or any future Commonwealth monetary unit will maintain a fixed relationship.

## 6. C$ Development Strategy

The Commonwealth envisions a phased financial architecture known publicly as **C$**.

### Phase I — Regulated Banking and Payment Rails

The initial CBA Wallet may use regulated banking, payment, or Banking-as-a-Service infrastructure.

Where underlying balances are legally U.S. dollars held through a regulated partner, the Commonwealth shall accurately disclose that fact. A branded C$ interface or accounting unit must not be misrepresented as an independently issued cryptocurrency when it is legally a dollar balance.

### Phase II — Regulated Stablecoin Infrastructure

After meaningful network activity exists, the Commonwealth may integrate or white-label an existing lawfully issued dollar stablecoin or comparable regulated digital settlement asset.

The Commonwealth may seek to retain control over the user experience, merchant network, economic data, incentives, and community layer to the extent contractually and legally permitted.

A representation that **1 C$ = 1 U.S. dollar of redeemable value** should be made only when the actual legal issuance and redemption structure supports that statement.

### Phase III — Permitted Commonwealth Dollar Issuance

Direct or affiliated issuance of C$ is a maturity-stage objective rather than a launch requirement.

A feasibility process should begin only after the Commonwealth has demonstrated sufficient:

- legal authority
- reserve capacity
- compliance systems
- capital
- cybersecurity
- governance
- audit readiness
- operational scale
- business justification

If C$ becomes a regulated payment stablecoin, required reserves shall be segregated, maintained in legally permitted assets, and subject to regular disclosure and redemption procedures.

Stablecoin reserve assets shall not be used for unrelated development spending.

## 7. Commonwealth Economic Index

The **Commonwealth Economic Index (CEI)** is intended to measure whether the participating economy is becoming more productive, interconnected, and capable of retaining economic activity.

Possible metrics include:

- **Gross Network Product (GNP-C)** — qualifying economic output attributable to participating enterprises under a published methodology.
- **Internal Circulation Ratio (ICR)** — qualifying purchases from participating merchants relative to total qualifying network spending.
- **Commonwealth Velocity (CV)** — qualifying transaction volume relative to average qualifying balances.
- **Economic Retention Rate (ERR)** — the share of measured funds entering the network that remain in participating accounts or are re-spent with participating merchants after a defined period.
- **Merchant Density** — active participating merchants per 1,000 active members.
- **Capital Formation** — net increase in qualifying productive assets, development-fund assets, cooperative equity, and approved business financing.

CEI is an economic measurement system. It shall not be represented as collateral for C$ unless a future lawful instrument expressly provides otherwise.

## 8. Economic Doctrine

> **C$ does not create the Commonwealth’s economic power. It is designed to make that power measurable, transferable, increasingly interconnected, and capable of supporting long-term institution-building.**

> **The currency is the rail. The economy is the power.**

---

**Status:** Public working framework. No statement in this document represents that C$ has already been issued, that deposits are presently insured through a CBA-controlled bank, or that the Commonwealth currently possesses authority to issue a regulated payment stablecoin.
