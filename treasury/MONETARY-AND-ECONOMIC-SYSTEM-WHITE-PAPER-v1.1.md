# Commonwealth Monetary & Economic System White Paper

**Commonwealth of Black America**  
**The Commonwealth Dollar (C$) - Reserve, Network, and Governance Architecture**  
**Version 1.1 - Banking / Program Partner Review Edition - August 2026**

> **FROM OUR HISTORY, WE BUILD OUR FUTURE.**

## Document Status

This is a planning and partner-review document. It is not a regulatory filing, legal opinion, securities offering, bank charter application, guarantee of launch timing, or representation that C$ has already been issued.

## 1. Executive Summary

The Commonwealth Dollar (C$) is a USD-referenced digital payment and economic-network concept designed to circulate within the Commonwealth of Black America's cooperative economic system. Its purpose is stability, payment infrastructure, merchant recirculation, and transparent economic measurement - not speculation.

In **Phase 1**, C$ is not intended to be a separately issued token. It is a brand and accounting layer over U.S.-dollar balances and payment rails provided through an appropriately structured licensed partner program.

The system separates the payment layer from the broader economic architecture. The merchant network, Commonwealth Economic Index (CEI), Development Fund, business-development programs, and recirculation strategy are the institutional layers through which the Commonwealth seeks to build durable economic capacity.

The recommended launch path is to begin with a bank, Banking-as-a-Service, or licensed program-manager structure in which custody, settlement, regulated payment functions, and allocated compliance duties remain with qualified partners under written agreements. Phase 2 may later integrate a third-party permitted payment stablecoin or comparable regulated digital settlement infrastructure. Direct C$ issuance should be evaluated only after the responsible Commonwealth entity can satisfy applicable approval, reserve, compliance, capital, governance, cybersecurity, reporting, and audit requirements.

### Current regulatory note - August 2026

The GENIUS Act was enacted July 18, 2025. OCC implementation rules referenced by this paper remain in proposed-rule stage as of this edition. The launch architecture must be re-validated against final rules, effective dates, partner requirements, and applicable state and federal payment laws before deployment.

## 2. Three-Layer Architecture

### 2.1 Layer 1 - C$ (Commonwealth Dollar)

The payment and accounting layer. In Phase 1, a user's displayed C$ balance is intended to correspond to U.S.-dollar funds held and settled through the partner program. The Commonwealth shall not describe Phase 1 C$ as an independently issued cryptocurrency, payment stablecoin, bank deposit, or insured product unless the actual legal structure supports that description.

### 2.2 Layer 2 - Customer Funds / Program Reserve Architecture

Customer funds are intended to be held by the partner bank or otherwise within the licensed program structure under the governing custody and program agreements. The Commonwealth may receive reporting data and contractual economic benefits, but shall not represent customer funds as Commonwealth-owned operating capital.

Any deposit-insurance statement must accurately describe the partner institution, account structure, eligibility conditions, and legal limits. Neither the CBA brand nor C$ itself should be described as FDIC insured.

### 2.3 Layer 3 - Commonwealth Development Instrument

A separate, non-transferable participation or utility instrument representing engagement with Commonwealth development programs. It is not intended to be sold to the public or represented as an investment. Any future transferable, saleable, profit-linked, or publicly offered version requires separate securities and financial-regulatory review before use.

## 3. What C$ Is in Each Phase

| Phase | Legal / operational substance | Who holds or issues value | Key rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Phase 1 - Partnered wallet** | Branded display and accounting of USD balances on partner payment rails; not a CBA-issued stablecoin. | Qualified bank / licensed program structure holds customer funds and executes allocated regulated functions. | Disclose the legal nature of balances accurately; customer funds are not development capital. |
| **Phase 2 - Third-party digital settlement** | CBA wallet may integrate a lawfully issued third-party dollar stablecoin or white-label digital settlement product. | Third-party permitted issuer remains issuer of record unless the approved structure provides otherwise. | 1:1 redemption and reserve claims must match the issuer's actual terms and regulatory status. |
| **Phase 3 - Direct / affiliated issuance** | C$ may become a separately issued payment stablecoin only after required approval and operational readiness. | CBA or affiliated entity only if accepted as an eligible permitted issuer under then-effective law. | Full reserve, redemption, risk, reporting, AML/sanctions, capital, governance, audit, and supervisory requirements apply. |

**The phase label does not determine legal status.** The actual contracts, custody, issuance, settlement, redemption, marketing, and compliance allocation determine what the product is.

## 4. Phase 1 Funds Flow

| Step | Funds-flow event | Control principle |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Member funds wallet | USD enters through an approved funding channel. |
| 2 | Partner receives / safeguards funds | Funds are credited within the partner program under the applicable custody structure. |
| 3 | CBA wallet displays C$ | The interface displays corresponding C$ as the accounting label for available USD value. |
| 4 | Member pays participating merchant | Partner/processor executes the payment; CBA records permitted transaction and CEI data. |
| 5 | Merchant recirculates or settles | Merchant may retain program value for network spending or redeem/settle to USD under partner terms. |

### Segregation principle

Customer balances, any payment-stablecoin reserves, the **$1 Founding Reserve**, operating funds, and Development Fund capital are distinct accounting and legal pools. They must not be commingled merely because they appear inside the same CBA-branded ecosystem.

## 5. Reserve, Issuance, and Redemption Mechanics

### 5.1 Phase 1 Funding / Credit

A member funds the wallet through a partner-approved channel. The partner program records the underlying USD value and the CBA interface displays the corresponding C$ amount. Phase 1 uses a crediting process rather than CBA stablecoin issuance.

### 5.2 Circulation

C$ may be used for partner-approved goods, services, peer-to-peer transfers, and other authorized payment use cases. Lending, remittances, stored-value cash loading, or other separately regulated products are outside the initial wallet scope unless a qualified partner and counsel expressly approve the product, licensing allocation, disclosures, and controls.

### 5.3 Redemption / Withdrawal

Phase 1 users withdraw or settle underlying USD according to partner terms, cutoffs, fees, fraud controls, and account rules. In a later stablecoin phase, redemption must comply with the then-effective rules governing the issuer and product.

### 5.4 Phase 2-3 Reserve Composition

If a payment stablecoin is integrated or directly issued, reserve assets must satisfy the applicable permitted-asset and redemption requirements of the issuer's regulatory framework. Commonwealth policy is full backing, high liquidity, short duration, no speculative leverage, and no use of payment reserves as collateral for unrelated Commonwealth obligations.

### 5.5 Reserve Yield and Economics

Interest, yield, rebates, or revenue associated with customer-funds structures or stablecoin reserves may be retained, shared, restricted, or unavailable depending on law, partner agreement, account type, reserve structure, and issuer status. Commonwealth budgets shall not assume access to such yield until that right has been confirmed in writing by the partner and counsel.

## 6. Commonwealth Economic Index (CEI)

The CEI measures the size, circulation, retention, and productive capacity of the Commonwealth economic network. It is **not** a currency-backing mechanism.

CEI may include:

- verified participating businesses and merchant count;
- annual merchant revenue processed through the network;
- C$ transaction volume and velocity;
- member balances and applicable reserve size;
- separately approved business-credit activity;
- Development Fund deployed capital and cooperative assets;
- employment supported through participating businesses;
- Economic Retention Rate and merchant-to-merchant recirculation.

The People's Assembly and Treasury governance bodies retain authority over Commonwealth budget, development, lending, and incentive policies. CEI is a published, auditable decision input, not an automatic monetary-policy trigger.

## 7. Merchant Network Economics

Participating merchants accept C$ according to the actual Phase 1 or later-phase legal structure. Merchants may keep value inside the network for future purchases or settle to USD under partner terms.

### Recirculation Effect

When merchants use received value with other participating businesses, the same underlying dollars may support multiple rounds of recorded transaction volume. This is a **velocity effect**, not an increase in the money supply.

### Network Revenue

The planning model uses a blended network fee of **0.35%** of qualifying merchant transaction volume. This is an illustrative program-revenue assumption, not a guaranteed fee or margin.

## 8. Governance and Responsibility Matrix

C$ operations are governed through the Commonwealth Treasury framework and a dedicated Payment Network Committee or successor body. Partner-program contracts must identify the entity responsible for each regulated function.

| Function | Phase 1 primary responsibility | CBA role / control | Required launch artifact |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Customer-funds custody | Partner bank / approved custodian | No use as operating capital; monitor reconciliation and disclosures | Account/custody agreement + funds-flow memo |
| Payment ledger & settlement | Partner bank / processor / program manager | Wallet UX, merchant network, permitted data and reconciliation oversight | Program agreement + reconciliation SOP |
| KYC / customer identification | Partner-led or contractually allocated | Approved data support, escalation and member communications | KYC allocation matrix + privacy notice |
| AML / transaction monitoring / sanctions | Partner-led for regulated program, duties allocated in writing | Operational support, case escalation, prohibited-use rules | AML/sanctions responsibility matrix |
| Fraud, disputes & error resolution | Allocated by product and applicable law | Front-line support if approved; evidence preservation and escalation | Dispute / complaint SOP |
| Merchant onboarding | Shared; partner approval where required | Recruitment, standards, training and community-program rules | Merchant agreement + prohibited-business policy |
| Consumer disclosures | Shared and counsel-reviewed | Branding must match legal substance | Approved terms, fee schedule and disclosures |
| Reserve / program transparency | Partner supplies authoritative balance data | Publish verified summaries and CEI reporting | Monthly transparency template |
| Lending / remittances | Not in launch scope unless separately approved | No launch until legal/partner gate is passed | Separate product counsel memo + partner approval |

## 10. Compliance and Launch Path

### Phase 1 - Partnered Pilot

**Illustrative target: 2-4 months after partner approval and onboarding.** This is an aspiration after a willing partner accepts the program, not a guarantee that diligence, bank approval, compliance onboarding, legal review, or integration can be completed within a fixed period.

Before pilot:

- select a qualified bank/program/processor structure;
- obtain a written funds-flow and regulatory-allocation memo from banking/fintech counsel;
- complete partner-approved KYC, AML/sanctions, fraud, privacy, complaint, dispute, cybersecurity, vendor and incident-response procedures;
- onboard a limited merchant cohort;
- test reconciliation, withdrawal/redemption, dispute, fraud and outage scenarios;
- publish the first verified program-transparency report after launch.

### Phase 2 - Third-Party Stablecoin / Digital Settlement

Integrate only a lawfully issued and appropriately regulated product after issuer, custody, redemption, disclosure, and program responsibilities are documented. Preserve clear separation between the CBA brand/economic network and the issuer of record.

### Phase 3 - Direct Issuance Evaluation

Evaluate **Federal qualified payment stablecoin issuer approval** or an eligible **State-qualified issuer** pathway under then-effective law. Complete capital, operational-backstop, reserve, redemption, audit, cybersecurity, BSA/AML, sanctions, risk-management, governance, reporting, and supervisory-readiness work before transition.

Internal Commonwealth resolutions or governance documents alone do not create issuer authority.

## 11. Reserve and Program Transparency Commitments

The Commonwealth intends to publish, no less than monthly and to the extent data may lawfully be disclosed and verified:

- legal nature of the C$ product and relevant partner/issuer roles;
- C$ / program balances or circulating supply, as applicable;
- customer-funds or reserve composition, where applicable;
- funding, withdrawals/redemptions and material settlement activity;
- network transaction volume and gross program revenue;
- partner/program fees and material operating expenses at an appropriate aggregate level;
- Development Fund deployment and Founding Reserve reporting on a separate basis;
- material incidents, service interruptions, and audit/attestation status where appropriate.

Phase 1 transparency must not imply that the Commonwealth itself holds a stablecoin reserve if the legal structure is customer USD balances held through a partner program.

## 13. Risk Factors and Legal Considerations

- **Regulatory characterization:** actual money-transmission, stored-value, deposit, payment, consumer-finance, or other status depends on final structure, contracts, flows, jurisdictions, and counsel analysis.
- **GENIUS Act implementation:** final rules and effective dates may alter Phase 2 or Phase 3 requirements.
- **Deposit insurance:** neither CBA nor C$ should be described as FDIC insured; any statement must accurately describe underlying eligible bank deposits and conditions.
- **Reserve / customer-funds economics:** yield, interest or program rebates cannot be assumed to belong to CBA until law and contract establish that right.
- **Securities risk:** the Development Instrument must remain non-transferable, unsold and free of investment-return promises unless separately reviewed.
- **Credit and remittance risk:** lending, credit, international remittance and similar products require separate approval.
- **Consumer protection:** disclosures, complaints, error resolution, fraud, chargebacks, account freezes, fees and customer-service obligations must be allocated under the actual product and law.
- **BSA/AML and sanctions:** duties must be contractually allocated and operationalized; direct issuance materially increases issuer obligations.
- **Cybersecurity and privacy:** the wallet creates material security, identity, transaction-data, vendor and incident-response responsibilities even when regulated functions are outsourced.
- **Partner concentration:** termination, policy changes, outages or risk decisions by a partner can materially affect Phase 1.
- **Scenario risk:** adoption, balances, transaction volume, merchant participation, pricing, yield and revenue may differ substantially from the illustrations.

> **Counsel-control clause:** No statement in this white paper overrides a bank program agreement, partner compliance requirement, final agency rule, applicable statute, court order, supervisory instruction, or written opinion of qualified counsel regarding the actual product structure.
