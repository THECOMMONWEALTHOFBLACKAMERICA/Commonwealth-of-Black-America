# Commonwealth of Black America

# THE CONSTITUTION — VISION EDITION v2

**FROM OUR HISTORY, WE BUILD OUR FUTURE.**

**Working Draft — For Founding Review and Future Ratification**

## Notice on Present Legal Status and Long-Term Aspiration

The Commonwealth of Black America is presently organized through voluntary institutions and legal entities operating under applicable law. Nothing in this Constitution independently creates territorial jurisdiction, diplomatic immunity, police powers, criminal-court jurisdiction, internationally recognized nationality, or authority to disregard the law of the United States or any other jurisdiction.

This notice shall not be construed as a renunciation of the Commonwealth’s lawful aspiration toward greater political, economic, cultural, institutional, or diplomatic self-determination. The Commonwealth may build institutions, seek partnerships, negotiate contracts and compacts, and pursue recognition or delegated authority through peaceful and lawful means.

Where an authority described in this Constitution requires legislation, governmental delegation, professional licensing, judicial recognition, accreditation, or another external legal act, the Commonwealth shall exercise that authority only after the required legal basis exists.

## Preamble

We, the Foundational Citizens of the Commonwealth of Black America, recognizing the enduring history, contributions, struggles, achievements, and cultural continuity of Black Americans, establish this Constitution to organize our people around justice, democratic self-government, historical preservation, economic cooperation, education, public responsibility, technological development, and institution-building.

We affirm that legitimate internal governance derives from the continuing consent and participation of the people. We therefore pledge to protect individual rights, reject corruption and arbitrary power, preserve our genealogical inheritance, build durable institutions for future generations, and pursue our aspirations through peaceful, lawful, democratic, and constitutional means.

## Article I — Character, Purpose, and Source of Authority

### Section 1 — Source of Internal Authority

The Commonwealth derives its internal authority from the voluntary consent of its Foundational Citizens and from this Constitution as ratified by them. No person shall be compelled to become or remain a citizen of the Commonwealth.

### Section 2 — Institutional Purpose

The Commonwealth exists to create durable civic, economic, educational, cultural, technological, charitable, and representative institutions for Foundational Black Americans, and to increase the capacity of its citizens and communities to organize, provide for themselves, preserve their history, build wealth, and govern Commonwealth affairs democratically.

### Section 3 — Long-Term Self-Government Strategy

The Commonwealth shall follow an institution-first strategy: build functioning institutions, document the constituency, demonstrate democratic legitimacy, develop economic capacity, form lawful relationships with existing governments and institutions, and seek negotiated recognition, delegation, partnership, or shared administration where appropriate.

### Section 4 — Relationship to Existing Governments

Commonwealth citizenship and institutions do not authorize violation or evasion of applicable law. Where the Commonwealth seeks governmental powers that it does not presently possess, it shall pursue those powers through legislation, contracts, municipal compacts, intergovernmental arrangements, lawful commissioning, accreditation, or other recognized legal mechanisms.

## Article II — Foundational Citizenship

### Section 1 — Eligibility

Foundational Citizenship shall be reserved for persons who can demonstrate direct ancestral lineage to Black American communities established in the United States before 1870.

### Section 2 — Evidence

Eligibility shall be established through documented genealogy rather than appearance or unsupported self-identification. Acceptable evidence may include federal and state census records, vital records, Freedmen’s Bureau records, slavery and emancipation records, church records, military records, court records, land and probate records, newspapers, family records, and other credible historical documentation.

### Section 3 — Citizenship Commission

A Foundational Citizenship Commission shall administer standards, review applications, protect confidential records, publish evidentiary guidance, preserve genealogical archives, and provide written reasons for decisions. Appeals shall be available to the Constitutional Court or designated appellate tribunal.

### Section 4 — Rights of Foundational Citizens

Foundational Citizens shall have the right to:

- Vote in Commonwealth elections and referenda.
- Seek and hold Commonwealth office subject to qualifications established by law.
- Petition, propose laws and constitutional amendments, and participate in Commonwealth institutions.
- Receive equal protection and due process under Commonwealth law.
- Access lawful Commonwealth programs subject to published eligibility rules.

### Section 5 — Citizenship Oath

> “I accept the responsibilities of citizenship within the Commonwealth of Black America. I pledge to uphold its Constitution, respect the rights of my fellow citizens, participate in democratic governance, preserve our history, and contribute to the prosperity and future of our people.”

### Section 6 — Non-Citizen Supporters

Persons who do not qualify for Foundational Citizenship may lawfully donate, volunteer, contract with, partner with, attend public programs of, or otherwise support Commonwealth institutions. Such support does not confer Commonwealth political citizenship or voting rights unless this Constitution is lawfully amended.

## Article III — Bill of Rights

Every Foundational Citizen is guaranteed:

- Human dignity and personal liberty.
- Freedom of speech, publication, thought, conscience, religion, and non-belief.
- Freedom of peaceful assembly and association.
- Equal protection under Commonwealth law.
- Notice, a fair hearing, and due process before deprivation of Commonwealth rights or status.
- Privacy in personal, genealogical, voting, and financial records, subject to lawful and narrowly tailored requirements.
- Property and contract rights within Commonwealth institutions, subject to law and due process.
- The right to vote, seek office, petition government, and participate in democratic decision-making.
- Protection against arbitrary punishment, secret proceedings, corruption, retaliation, and abuse of office.
- The right to inspect public Commonwealth records, budgets, audits, and final governmental decisions subject to lawful privacy protections.
- The right to propose constitutional reform through the procedures established in this Constitution.

## Article IV — Structure of Government

### Section 1 — Three Branches

The Commonwealth government shall consist of an Executive Branch, a People’s Assembly, and a Constitutional Court. No branch may exercise powers assigned to another except as expressly authorized by this Constitution.

### Section 2 — Executive Branch

The Executive Branch shall be led by a Chancellor and shall administer departments authorized by the People’s Assembly, including Treasury and Economic Development; Justice; Education and Knowledge; Health and Community Wellness; Technology and Innovation; Infrastructure and Development; Public Safety and Emergency Management; and Foreign Affairs.

### Section 3 — People’s Assembly

The People’s Assembly shall enact laws, approve budgets, authorize departments, ratify major compacts and treaties of institutional cooperation, oversee executive administration, and represent the Foundational Citizens.

### Section 4 — Constitutional Court

The Constitutional Court shall interpret this Constitution, protect the Bill of Rights, review internal governmental disputes, hear citizenship and election appeals, and supervise the integrity of Commonwealth judicial and arbitral institutions.

### Section 5 — Civil Service

A professional, merit-based Civil Service shall administer records, programs, technology, finance, elections, public services, and other operations on a nonpartisan basis.

## Article V — Progressive Ranked Democracy and Continuous Accountability

### Section 1 — Ranked Elections

Commonwealth elections shall use ranked ballots where practicable. Voters may rank candidates or parties first, second, third, and onward. The Election Code shall establish transparent transfer, elimination, or tabulation rules designed to identify the choice with the broadest democratic support.

### Section 2 — One Citizen, One Vote

Each verified Foundational Citizen shall have one vote in each election for which that citizen is eligible. Voting systems shall be auditable, accessible, secure, and designed to protect ballot secrecy while preventing duplicate voting.

### Section 3 — Continuous Public Confidence

Elected executive and legislative officers shall be subject to a continuously updated public-confidence system. The Election Code shall establish minimum participation thresholds, anti-manipulation safeguards, cooling-off periods, due process, and recall or special-election procedures.

### Section 4 — Transparency

Final election totals, ranking distributions, public-confidence results, audits, and adjudicated election disputes shall be preserved in a tamper-evident public archive while protecting ballot secrecy.

## Article VI — Treasury, Community Development, and the Permanent Reserve

### Section 1 — Treasury

The Commonwealth Treasury shall receive and manage lawful dues, membership contributions, grants, donations, program revenue, enterprise distributions, investment income, and other authorized revenue. Treasury accounts shall be subject to budgets, segregation of duties, public reporting, conflict-of-interest rules, and independent review or audit.

### Section 2 — Community Development Fund

The Commonwealth shall maintain a Community Development Fund for programs and capital projects including Black community schools and educational programs, community centers, grocery stores and food cooperatives, food banks, housing and neighborhood development, business formation, technology, health and wellness initiatives, cultural preservation, and other projects approved by law.

### Section 3 — One-Dollar Founding Reserve Contribution

Upon admission, each Foundational Citizen shall make a one-time Founding Reserve Contribution of one United States dollar ($1.00). The minimum book-value floor of the Founding Reserve Fund shall not intentionally fall below one dollar multiplied by the number of Foundational Citizens whose citizenship has been formally admitted and not terminated, except to correct accounting error, fraud, or a legally required charge.

### Section 4 — Reserve Separation

The Founding Reserve Fund shall be accounted for separately from routine operating funds and from donor-restricted program funds. It shall not be used for ordinary operating expenses. The Treasury Charter shall establish custody, liquidity, audit, and emergency rules consistent with this constitutional floor.

### Section 5 — Gold Reserve Objective

As the Commonwealth’s financial capacity grows, the Treasury shall pursue a long-term objective of holding allocated physical gold or other approved hard-reserve assets with market value equal to or greater than the constitutional Founding Reserve floor. Gold is a reserve objective, not a promise that the market price of gold or any Commonwealth monetary unit will remain fixed.

### Section 6 — Donations and Public Support

Donations from citizens and non-citizens may support Commonwealth institutions and community programs. Donor restrictions shall be honored where accepted. No officer shall represent a donation as tax-deductible unless the receiving entity is legally eligible to issue such a receipt.

## Article VII — Commonwealth Monetary and Economic System

### Section 1 — Purpose

The Commonwealth shall develop payment, banking, data, and monetary infrastructure to make participating economic activity measurable, transferable, increasingly interconnected, and capable of supporting long-term institution-building. The currency is the rail; productive economic organization is the source of leverage.

### Section 2 — Phase I: Regulated Banking and Payment Rails

The initial CBA Wallet may operate on regulated banking, payments, or Banking-as-a-Service infrastructure. Where the underlying balance is legally United States dollars held through a regulated partner, the Commonwealth shall accurately disclose that status and shall not misrepresent a branded balance as an independently issued cryptocurrency.

### Section 3 — Phase II: Regulated Stablecoin Infrastructure

After sufficient network activity exists, the Commonwealth may integrate or white-label an existing lawfully issued dollar stablecoin or comparable regulated digital settlement asset, while retaining control over the Commonwealth user experience, merchant network, economic data, and community features to the extent contractually permitted.

### Section 4 — Phase III: Permitted Commonwealth Dollar Issuance

The People’s Assembly may authorize a feasibility process for direct or affiliated issuance of a Commonwealth Dollar (C$) only after appropriate legal authority, reserves, compliance capacity, cybersecurity, governance, audit readiness, and capital requirements have been satisfied. If C$ is issued as a payment stablecoin, required reserves shall be segregated and maintained in legally permitted assets, with regular disclosure and redemption procedures.

### Section 5 — Commonwealth Economic Index

The Commonwealth Economic Index (CEI) shall measure the participating economy using published methods that may include Gross Network Product, Internal Circulation Ratio, Commonwealth Velocity, Economic Retention Rate, Merchant Density, Capital Formation, business participation, employment, and other relevant indicators. CEI is an economic measurement system and shall not be represented as reserve collateral for C$ unless a future lawful instrument expressly provides otherwise.

### Section 6 — Development Capital

Customer funds, payment reserves, and stablecoin reserves shall not be commingled with development capital. Development investment shall be conducted through separately authorized funds or entities with appropriate governance and legal review.

## Article VIII — Community Institutions and Self-Reliance

### Section 1 — Institutional Mission

The Commonwealth shall seek to build durable institutions capable of continuing essential community functions regardless of changes in federal, state, or local public spending. The purpose is greater community capacity and resilience, not unlawful withdrawal from existing civic obligations.

### Section 2 — Education

The Commonwealth may establish, support, fund, or partner with lawful schools, tutoring programs, academies, vocational programs, apprenticeships, universities, research institutions, libraries, archives, and digital learning systems serving its mission.

### Section 3 — Food and Community Infrastructure

The Commonwealth may establish or support grocery stores, food cooperatives, food banks, community kitchens, agricultural programs, community centers, youth facilities, senior programs, emergency relief systems, and other institutions supporting food security and neighborhood stability.

### Section 4 — Economic Development

The Commonwealth may support business formation, merchant networks, cooperative purchasing, property ownership, construction, housing, technology, employment, trade, capital access, and procurement among participating institutions and businesses.

### Section 5 — Public Benefit and Nondiscrimination in Service

Programs may be designed to advance the Commonwealth’s mission and Foundational Citizens while complying with applicable civil-rights, charitable, contractual, licensing, and public-accommodation requirements. Where law or funding conditions require broader eligibility, programs shall be administered accordingly.

## Article IX — Local Commonwealth Governments and Municipal Compacts

### Section 1 — Local and Regional Organization

Foundational Citizens may organize Local Commonwealth Chapters and Regional Commonwealth Councils under standards enacted by the People’s Assembly. Local bodies shall elect or otherwise democratically select their officers and shall remain accountable to this Constitution.

### Section 2 — Local Administrative Functions

Local bodies may administer lawful community programs, property, economic-development initiatives, education, food systems, emergency assistance, mediation, cultural institutions, data collection, public meetings, and other functions delegated by Commonwealth law.

### Section 3 — Municipal Compact Strategy

When a local or regional Commonwealth institution has sufficient membership, governance, operational capacity, financial controls, and public support, it may seek a written compact, contract, memorandum of understanding, franchise, delegation, or other lawful agreement with a city, county, state, school district, public authority, or other governmental body.

### Section 4 — Scope of Negotiated Agreements

Subject to applicable law, negotiated agreements may concern education, community development, housing, food security, public-safety partnerships, restorative justice, mediation, neighborhood services, cultural affairs, infrastructure, workforce development, data-sharing, emergency response, or other public functions.

### Section 5 — No Unilateral Governmental Power

A Local or Regional Commonwealth body shall not exercise coercive governmental authority over non-consenting persons merely by adopting a Commonwealth ordinance. Governmental powers over the public shall be exercised only when supported by valid law, contract, delegation, commissioning, consent, or other recognized authority.

## Article X — Justice, Mediation, Arbitration, and Regional Tribunals

### Section 1 — Commonwealth Judicial System

The Commonwealth may establish local hearing bodies, Regional Tribunals, appellate panels, and the Constitutional Court to adjudicate internal matters arising under Commonwealth law, including citizenship, elections, officer conduct, contracts, membership discipline, and disputes among Commonwealth institutions.

### Section 2 — Voluntary Mediation and Arbitration

Regional Tribunals may provide mediation, restorative-justice conferences, and arbitration for private disputes when the parties voluntarily consent or are bound by a lawful agreement. The Justice Code shall establish neutrality, notice, conflicts rules, evidence standards, representation rights, written decisions, and appeal procedures.

### Section 3 — Enforcement

Where an arbitral award or mediated agreement requires enforcement outside the Commonwealth, enforcement shall proceed through the courts or other mechanisms recognized by applicable law.

### Section 4 — Future Public Jurisdiction

The Commonwealth may seek agreements or legislation authorizing its tribunals to receive referrals, administer diversion or restorative-justice programs, conduct recognized arbitration or administrative hearings, or perform other public functions. Criminal conviction, imprisonment, compulsory subpoena, contempt powers against the public, or involuntary jurisdiction shall not be exercised without lawful governmental authorization.

### Section 5 — Judicial Independence

Judges and tribunal officers shall be protected from improper political influence, required to disclose conflicts, and removable only through procedures established by law and consistent with due process.

## Article XI — Public Safety, Emergency Management, and Negotiated Policing Authority

### Section 1 — Community Safety Corps

The Commonwealth may establish a Community Safety Corps for lawful functions such as safety education, event safety, neighborhood observation, escorts, missing-person assistance, de-escalation, conflict intervention, emergency preparedness, disaster relief, first-aid coordination, and other services permitted by law.

### Section 2 — Licensed Security

Where the Commonwealth provides private security services, armed security, investigations, emergency medical services, or other regulated functions, personnel and entities shall obtain the licenses, training, insurance, and authority required by applicable law.

### Section 3 — Municipal Public-Safety Compacts

Local and Regional Commonwealth governments may seek contracts, commissions, special-service arrangements, co-response programs, civilian-oversight agreements, or other lawful partnerships with municipalities and other governments for public-safety services.

### Section 4 — Police Powers

No Commonwealth officer shall exercise arrest powers, traffic-enforcement authority, criminal-investigation powers, access to restricted government databases, warrant authority, compulsory detention, or other governmental police powers unless those powers have been lawfully granted by the competent government and the officer meets all applicable certification and training requirements.

### Section 5 — Accountability

Any future Commonwealth police or public-safety body exercising delegated governmental authority shall be subject to civilian oversight, public rules, use-of-force standards, complaint procedures, independent investigation, data reporting, body-camera or comparable accountability requirements where applicable, and judicial review.

### Section 6 — No Unauthorized Militia

The Commonwealth shall not maintain an armed force or militia exercising governmental authority without a lawful basis. Nothing in this Section restricts lawful individual rights, licensed security, emergency-response functions, or any future public-safety authority validly granted by law.

## Article XII — Foreign Affairs and External Recognition

### Section 1 — Foreign Affairs Office

A Department of Foreign Affairs shall conduct cultural, educational, economic, trade, academic, archival, diaspora, and institutional outreach and shall maintain professional standards for Commonwealth representatives.

### Section 2 — External Recognition Strategy

The Commonwealth may lawfully seek meetings, cultural agreements, memoranda of understanding, observer relationships, institutional partnerships, trade dialogue, research cooperation, and other forms of external engagement. It may express a long-term aspiration for broader recognition while accurately describing its present status.

### Section 3 — Diplomatic Status

Commonwealth titles and credentials do not independently create diplomatic immunity, consular privilege, internationally recognized passports, diplomatic license plates, or foreign-government accreditation. Such status exists only when a competent external government or international body lawfully grants or recognizes it.

## Article XIII — Amendments, Initiatives, and Referenda

### Section 1 — Amendment Proposal

Amendments may be proposed by a two-thirds vote of the People’s Assembly, by a citizen petition meeting a threshold established by the Election Code, or by a Constitutional Convention lawfully called under this Constitution.

### Section 2 — Public Review and Ratification

Proposed amendments shall receive a public review period and shall be ratified by Foundational Citizens through a verified vote using the standards established by the Election Code.

### Section 3 — Entrenched Principles

No amendment may abolish democratic government, the Bill of Rights, due process, peaceful transfer of office, equal protection under Commonwealth law, the voluntary nature of citizenship, or the requirement that powers dependent on external legal authority be exercised only when lawfully obtained.

## Article XIV — General Provisions

### Section 1 — Constitutional Supremacy Within the Commonwealth

This Constitution is the supreme internal governing instrument of the Commonwealth. Commonwealth statutes, codes, charters, policies, compacts, contracts, and actions shall be interpreted consistently with it.

### Section 2 — Applicable External Law

The Commonwealth and its institutions shall comply with applicable external law. If an internal rule conflicts with controlling external law, the internal rule shall be unenforceable to the extent of the conflict unless and until lawful authority changes that result.

### Section 3 — Severability

If a provision is held invalid or unenforceable, the remaining provisions shall remain in effect to the greatest extent possible.

### Section 4 — Conforming Companion Documents

Upon ratification of this Vision Edition v2, the Bylaws, Citizenship Code, Election Code, Treasury Charter, Foreign Affairs Manual, Diplomatic Protocol Manual, Identity Standards Manual, and future Local Government, Justice, Public Safety, and Municipal Compact Codes shall be conformed to this Constitution. Any inconsistent prior provision shall be revised before final adoption.

### Section 5 — Effective Date

This Constitution shall take effect upon ratification by the Foundational Citizens according to the founding ratification procedure adopted for that purpose.

## Founding Ratification

We, the undersigned Foundational Citizens, ratify this Constitution as the governing charter of the Commonwealth of Black America, subject to the Notice on Present Legal Status and Long-Term Aspiration and to applicable law.
