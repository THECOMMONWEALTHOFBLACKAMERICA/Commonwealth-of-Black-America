# External Engagement and Recognition Policy

**Commonwealth of Black America**  
**Public Working Framework — August 8, 2026**

## Purpose

The Department of Foreign Affairs is intended to conduct professional cultural, educational, economic, trade, academic, archival, diaspora, and institutional outreach on behalf of the Commonwealth.

The long-term objective is to build a documented record of real membership, functioning institutions, democratic legitimacy, economic capacity, and durable external relationships capable of supporting greater political, cultural, economic, institutional, or diplomatic self-determination over time.

## Present Status

The Commonwealth may lawfully seek meetings, cultural agreements, memoranda of understanding, observer relationships, institutional partnerships, trade dialogue, research cooperation, and other forms of external engagement.

The Commonwealth may state its long-term aspiration for broader recognition while accurately describing its present legal status.

Commonwealth titles and credentials do not independently create diplomatic immunity, consular privilege, internationally recognized passports, diplomatic license plates, foreign-government accreditation, or exemption from applicable law.

Any such status exists only when a competent external government or international body lawfully grants or recognizes it.

## Permitted Areas of Engagement

External relations may include:

- cultural exchange
- historical and genealogical research
- university and archival partnerships
- educational exchanges
- trade and economic dialogue
- nonprofit and civil-society partnerships
- diaspora engagement
- institutional cooperation
- international conferences
- human-rights and self-determination forums
- research and scholarship

## Recognition Strategy

The Commonwealth’s recognition strategy is institution-first rather than declaration-first.

The sequence is:

1. Build documented membership and credible citizenship standards.
2. Establish functioning democratic governance.
3. Develop transparent Treasury and economic institutions.
4. Build lawful community institutions and programs.
5. Form professional relationships with domestic and international institutions.
6. Document public support, institutional capacity, and successful partnerships.
7. Seek increasingly formal relationships, observer roles, compacts, recognition, or delegated authority where peaceful and lawful opportunities exist.

## Representation Standards

No representative should misstate the Commonwealth’s current legal status to a government, international organization, institution, business, or member of the public.

Authorized representatives should clearly distinguish between:

- the Commonwealth’s present voluntary and institutional status;
- its internal constitutional offices and titles; and
- any governmental, diplomatic, consular, or public authority actually granted by an external competent authority.

## External Agreements

Any memorandum, compact, partnership agreement, or other institutional commitment should identify:

- the parties
- the purpose
- the legal capacity in which each party acts
- financial obligations
- duration
- termination rights
- reporting obligations
- governing law where appropriate
- approval or ratification requirements

Major external agreements should be subject to Commonwealth review and ratification procedures established by the Constitution and future Foreign Affairs Code.

## Long-Term Principle

> **The Commonwealth seeks to become more credible by becoming more real: real citizens, real institutions, real democratic records, real economic activity, real programs, and real partnerships.**

Broader recognition, if pursued, is the possible result of sustained institution-building rather than a substitute for it.
