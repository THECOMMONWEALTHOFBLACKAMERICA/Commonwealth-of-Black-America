# COMMONWEALTH OF BLACK AMERICA

## PUBLIC NOTICE ON FBA IDENTITY, MISREPRESENTATION, ENTRAPMENT, COLLECTIVE LIABILITY & UNLAWFUL TARGETING

**August 8, 2026**

**Issued by the Commonwealth of Black America**

> **FROM OUR HISTORY, WE BUILD OUR FUTURE.**

## 1. Foundational Black American Identity Is Distinct From Any Organization

**Foundational Black American (FBA)** is a historical and lineage-based identity referring generally to Black Americans whose ancestral communities were established in the United States through the historical experiences of slavery, emancipation, Reconstruction, and subsequent generations of American life.

FBA is **not synonymous with the Commonwealth of Black America**, nor is the FBA population itself a single organization, political party, activist group, corporation, movement, or command structure.

The **Commonwealth of Black America (CBA)** is a separate voluntary civic and institutional organization whose Foundational Citizenship standard is based upon documented pre-1870 Black American ancestry.

Participation in the Commonwealth is voluntary.

No officer, citizen, influencer, organization, political commentator, or public figure possesses authority to speak for every person of Foundational Black American ancestry.

Similarly, the actions or statements of one person identifying as FBA shall not automatically be attributed to other FBAs or to the Commonwealth.

## 2. Rejection of Collective Guilt and Collective Criminalization

The Commonwealth rejects any attempt to impose **collective responsibility, collective suspicion, or collective criminal liability** upon Foundational Black Americans because of ancestry, identity, lawful speech, political opinions, cultural affiliation, or the independent conduct of unrelated individuals.

Criminal responsibility in the United States ordinarily attaches to the conduct, intent, and legally provable actions of particular persons—not to an ancestral population as a whole.

The Commonwealth likewise rejects the use of isolated social-media statements, anonymous accounts, unrelated criminal activity, or the conduct of self-appointed spokespersons as evidence that millions of Americans sharing a lineage identity constitute a criminal or extremist organization.

Nothing about identifying as FBA, standing alone, establishes participation in unlawful activity.

## 3. Distinction Between the Commonwealth and Independent Speakers

The Commonwealth maintains its own Constitution, governance documents, policies, officers, public records, and authorized statements.

A person does **not** speak for the Commonwealth merely because that person:

- identifies as FBA;
- discusses FBA issues online;
- uses terminology also used by Commonwealth citizens;
- supports some Commonwealth goals;
- criticizes government policy;
- advocates Black American economic development or self-determination; or
- claims an association with the Commonwealth without authorization.

Official Commonwealth positions must be established through authorized Commonwealth institutions or clearly identified Commonwealth communications.

## 4. Protection of Lawful Speech, Assembly and Association

Foundational Black Americans possess the same constitutional protections available to other persons in the United States.

The First Amendment protects speech, peaceful assembly, petitioning of government, and forms of expressive association. Government generally may not suppress expression merely because officials disagree with its ideas or message.

Accordingly, advocacy concerning **Black American history, lineage, reparative policy, economic organization, political representation, community institutions, governmental reform, self-determination, or peaceful constitutional change** should not, without additional lawful grounds, be treated as evidence of criminal intent.

## 5. Government Investigations and Retaliatory Targeting

The Commonwealth recognizes the legitimate authority of law-enforcement agencies to investigate actual violations of law.

At the same time, government authority must remain subject to constitutional limitations.

The Commonwealth opposes investigations, surveillance, retaliation, intimidation, disruption, or adverse governmental action undertaken **solely because of lawful speech, lawful association, ancestry, political viewpoint, or peaceful participation in Commonwealth institutions.**

For state and local actors, **42 U.S.C. § 1983** provides a civil remedy in qualifying circumstances when a person acting under color of state law deprives another person of federally protected rights.

Other civil-rights statutes may apply depending upon the facts of a particular case. The applicability of any statute must be determined case by case rather than presumed.

## 6. Entrapment, Provocation and Manufactured Criminality

The Commonwealth specifically rejects efforts by any person to manufacture criminal conduct through provocation, impersonation, fabricated evidence, false-flag activity, or attempts to encourage Commonwealth citizens to engage in violence or illegal activity.

Citizens and officers are instructed to reject individuals who encourage:

- violence;
- threats;
- illegal weapons activity;
- destruction of property;
- fraud;
- criminal conspiracies;
- attacks upon government personnel or civilians;
- racial violence;
- unauthorized militia activity; or
- other illegal conduct.

Under federal law, **entrapment** is a specific criminal-law doctrine and should not be used loosely. It generally concerns government inducement of an offense and whether the accused was predisposed to commit that offense. Merely encountering a provocateur or deceptive private account does not automatically establish legal entrapment.

The Commonwealth nevertheless reserves the right to document and expose impersonation, fabricated communications, coordinated provocation, fraudulent attribution, or unlawful government conduct where credible evidence establishes that such conduct occurred.

## 7. False Accounts, Impersonation and Digital Manipulation

No anonymous account, parody account, artificial-intelligence-generated communication, impersonator, infiltrator, provocateur, or unauthorized individual shall be presumed to represent the Commonwealth.

Official Commonwealth communications should be verified through established Commonwealth channels and public records.

Where credible evidence shows that a person has intentionally fabricated Commonwealth communications, impersonated Commonwealth officers, falsified records, or falsely attributed violent or criminal statements to the Commonwealth, the organization may seek appropriate platform, administrative, civil, or legal remedies.

## 8. Misrepresentation by Media, Researchers and Public Institutions

The Commonwealth supports legitimate journalism, scholarship, criticism, investigation, and public debate.

It simultaneously requests that journalists, researchers, government institutions, technology companies, civil-rights organizations, and intelligence or law-enforcement bodies distinguish among:

- **FBA as a lineage identity;**
- **individual persons who identify as FBA;**
- **independent FBA-oriented organizations; and**
- **the Commonwealth of Black America as a particular voluntary institution.**

These categories are not interchangeable.

Statements made by one individual should not be attributed to an entire ancestral population without factual justification.

Likewise, claims regarding Commonwealth policies should be checked against official Commonwealth documents whenever reasonably possible.

## 9. Hate Crimes and Identity-Based Violence

Federal hate-crime law, including **18 U.S.C. § 249**, addresses specified acts involving bodily injury or attempted bodily injury motivated by characteristics such as actual or perceived race, color, religion, national origin, gender, sexual orientation, gender identity, or disability under the circumstances defined by statute.

Section 249 should therefore **not** be described as a general remedy for unfavorable media coverage, online criticism, misinformation, or ordinary defamation.

However, actual violence directed against persons because of a legally protected characteristic may implicate federal or state hate-crime laws depending upon the facts.

## 10. Defamation and False Attribution

The Commonwealth reserves all rights available under applicable law when knowingly or negligently false factual statements cause legally cognizable harm.

This may include circumstances involving false assertions concerning:

- criminal conduct;
- financial fraud;
- violent activity;
- organizational affiliation;
- statements falsely attributed to Commonwealth officials;
- fabricated Commonwealth documents; or
- materially false descriptions of Commonwealth operations.

Whether a statement constitutes actionable defamation depends upon applicable law, the speaker, the subject, whether the assertion is fact or opinion, applicable privileges, fault requirements, and other circumstances.

Political disagreement, criticism, satire, and unfavorable opinion are not automatically defamation.

## 11. No Advocacy of Violence or Unlawful Overthrow

The Commonwealth's institution-building strategy is:

> **BUILD → DEMONSTRATE → NEGOTIATE → INSTITUTIONALIZE**

The Commonwealth pursues its objectives through peaceful organization, democratic participation, commerce, education, genealogy, institution-building, contracts, legislation, municipal agreements, lawful advocacy, diplomacy, and other constitutional means.

The Commonwealth does not authorize terrorism, racial violence, criminal coercion, political assassination, illegal armed activity, or violence against civilians or government personnel.

Persons advocating such conduct do not acquire Commonwealth authorization merely by using CBA or FBA terminology.

## 12. No Presumption From Political or Institutional Ambition

Advocating substantial political change is not itself equivalent to advocating violence.

The Commonwealth may lawfully discuss and advocate subjects including:

- constitutional reform;
- community self-government;
- municipal agreements;
- delegated governmental authority;
- economic autonomy;
- reparative policies;
- independent institutions;
- international engagement;
- recognition;
- political restructuring; and
- peaceful self-determination.

Such proposals should be evaluated according to their actual content rather than being automatically characterized as criminal simply because they contemplate significant political or institutional change.

## 13. Documentation and Response Protocol

Where the Commonwealth reasonably believes that it or its citizens have been materially misrepresented, impersonated, falsely accused, or unlawfully targeted, the preferred response shall be institutional rather than retaliatory.

The Commonwealth may:

1. preserve evidence;
2. request correction or clarification;
3. publish the authentic Commonwealth record;
4. submit administrative or platform complaints;
5. make lawful public-record requests;
6. consult qualified counsel;
7. seek judicial relief where a valid claim exists; and
8. maintain a permanent historical record of significant incidents.

Threats, harassment, vigilantism, or retaliation are prohibited.

## 14. Institutional Independence

The Commonwealth does not claim authority over every Foundational Black American.

Nor does an individual's rejection of the Commonwealth affect that person's ancestry or right to identify with their own family history.

**CBA citizenship is an institutional status.**

**FBA ancestry is a lineage question.**

The two concepts must not be deliberately conflated.

## 15. Reservation of Rights

Nothing in this notice waives any constitutional, statutory, contractual, civil, organizational, or other lawful right available to the Commonwealth or to any individual.

Nothing herein should be interpreted to interfere with legitimate law enforcement based upon individualized evidence of criminal activity.

Instead, this Notice establishes a fundamental Commonwealth principle:

> **Ancestry is not criminal affiliation. Political thought is not criminal conduct. Lawful association is not conspiracy. Individual wrongdoing does not create collective guilt.**
