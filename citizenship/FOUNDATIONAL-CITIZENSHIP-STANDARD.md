# Foundational Citizenship Standard

**Commonwealth of Black America**  
**Public Working Standard — August 8, 2026**

## 1. Purpose

Foundational Citizenship is the political citizenship class of the Commonwealth of Black America. It is intended to preserve documented historical continuity with Black American communities established in the United States before 1870.

## 2. Eligibility

An applicant must demonstrate direct ancestral lineage to a Black American community established in the United States before 1870.

Eligibility is determined through documented genealogy rather than appearance, unsupported self-identification, or family tradition alone.

Historical records may identify qualifying ancestors using classifications such as:

- Black
- Colored
- Mulatto
- Free Person of Color
- African
- African American
- comparable historical classifications indicating Black American ancestry in the United States

## 3. Required Lineage Submission

A complete application should identify the claimed ancestral line generation by generation from the applicant to the qualifying ancestor.

The applicant should provide credible documentary support for each generational link and at least one record placing a qualifying direct ancestor within the pre-1870 historical standard or, where such a record does not survive, the earliest credible evidence consistent with continuous community membership.

## 4. Evidence

Evidence may include:

### Strong primary evidence

- Federal and state census records
- Birth, marriage, and death records
- Freedmen’s Bureau records
- Manumission and emancipation records
- Slavery-era records
- Church and sacramental records
- Probate and estate records
- Land and property records

### Supporting evidence

- Military service and pension records
- Court records
- Tax records
- Newspaper records
- Cemetery and mortuary records
- Family Bibles with contemporaneous entries
- Other credible historical records

### Corroborating evidence

- DNA match clustering consistent with the claimed line
- Recorded oral history
- Published genealogies and family histories

DNA evidence may corroborate a paper-trail lineage but should not, standing alone, establish Foundational Citizenship because DNA by itself does not establish the specific pre-1870 U.S. community-history requirement.

## 5. Foundational Citizenship Commission

The Foundational Citizenship Commission is intended to:

- Review applications and supporting documentation.
- Maintain consistent evidentiary standards.
- Protect confidential genealogical and identity records.
- Preserve historical records and genealogical archives.
- Provide written reasons for approvals, requests for additional evidence, and denials.
- Establish an appeal path to the Constitutional Court or designated appellate tribunal.

Until the Commission is formally constituted, founding enrollments may be recorded as **provisional founding enrollments pending formal Commission ratification**.

## 6. Rights of Foundational Citizens

Foundational Citizens may, subject to the Constitution and Commonwealth law:

- Vote in Commonwealth elections and referenda.
- Seek and hold Commonwealth office.
- Petition and propose laws and constitutional amendments.
- Participate in Commonwealth institutions.
- Receive equal protection and due process under Commonwealth law.
- Access lawful Commonwealth programs subject to published eligibility standards.

## 7. Citizenship Oath

> “I accept the responsibilities of citizenship within the Commonwealth of Black America. I pledge to uphold its Constitution, respect the rights of my fellow citizens, participate in democratic governance, preserve our history, and contribute to the prosperity and future of our people.”

## 8. Non-Citizen Supporters

People who do not qualify for Foundational Citizenship may donate, volunteer, contract with, partner with, or participate in public programs of Commonwealth institutions where lawful and appropriate. Such participation does not confer political citizenship or voting rights.

## 9. Privacy

Private citizenship files should not be published in the public registry. Public entries should normally contain only the citizen’s name, registry number, classification, enrollment date, public status, and a brief eligibility statement.

Birth certificates, addresses, signatures, government identification numbers, raw DNA data, financial information, private information about living relatives, and complete genealogy files should remain in secure records.

## 10. Public Registry Format

Public registry numbers use the format:

`CBA-FC-000001`
